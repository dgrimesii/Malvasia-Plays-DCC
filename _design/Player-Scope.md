# Player Scope

What the player surface is for, what it deliberately is not, and how to describe it to someone who has never seen it.

Player-side counterpart to [[Scope]]. Governed by [[Constraint-Serves-The-Table]].

---

## What it replaces

The player surface is not new capability. **It replaces a worse tool that is already at the table** — handwritten notes, a Google Doc, a folder of session summaries, a phone photo of a map.

That changes the bar it has to clear. The comparison is not *tool versus nothing*; it is *tool versus flipping through a notebook*. And the notebook is already an interruption: it takes longer, it fails more often, and it removes the player from the conversation for the whole duration of the search.

**A lookup that succeeds in three seconds is less disruptive than the thing it replaces.** So the memory bank is not in tension with [[Constraint-Serves-The-Table]] — it is one of the clearest wins available under it.

---

## Positioning: three horns, and the invisible one

The customer-facing articulation. A player at the table, half-remembering something, has three options and all three cost something real.

| Option | What you get | What you pay |
|---|---|---|
| **Flip through notes** | the right answer | table time and everyone's attention; the scene deflates |
| **Trust memory** | momentum | the risk of being wrong |
| **Don't ask** | nothing visible | the contribution never happens |

The second horn is worse than it looks. A misremembered detail spoken confidently enters the group's shared record, and either has to be walked back later or never is.

**The third is the largest and the only one with no symptom.** The player who judges the interruption not worth it and stays quiet costs the table something nobody ever sees — not the GM, not the group, not the player afterward. This is the strongest form of the pitch, because it moves the product from *convenience* to **contributions that would not otherwise have happened.**

The claim: the choice was **false all along**. It is an artefact of the retrieval medium, not of playing the game. Remove the cost of looking and there is nothing to trade.

### How to pitch it, and how not to

**Pitch it to the player who does not have the encyclopedic memory** — which is most people, and includes the quiet player whom GM-observed **Investment** already under-reads per [[Live-Set]]. Framed that way it is about **participation**, not efficiency.

**Do not pitch it as *never forget anything*.** That is a completeness promise: unachievable, and the wrong appeal. It also sells the thing [[Constraint-Serves-The-Table]] prohibits.

**Keep the emphasis on *check quickly and get back to the conversation*, never *the record is where the game lives*.** Getting this wrong in the marketing would promise a product the design refuses to build.

It maps onto the Warp & Weft metaphor directly: the players each hold threads they cannot see the whole of, and the tool is what lets them find their own thread without stopping the loom.

## The boundary: where players check, not where they think

A real scope boundary, not a deferral.

| Stays outside the tool | Belongs in the tool |
|---|---|
| Speculation and theorising | What the party knows, retrievable in the moment |
| Player character development and interiority | Names, connections, past events, who said what |
| Deep session notes in the player's own voice | The answer to a question asked at the table |

Deep notes live wherever the player likes. That is generative work, done away from the table, on their own terms, in their own medium. **A player who keeps a speculation doc in Google Docs is not doing something the tool should fix.**

This is the same shape as the GM side: per [[Modes-and-Surfaces]], the GM plans outside the tool and the tool holds what is needed in the moment. Speculation and character development are the player-side equivalent.

### Consequence for future player notes

When player-authored capture arrives, it must **not** try to absorb the deep notes. The target is much narrower: the small subset a player would want retrievable at the table.

And per [[Constraint-Serves-The-Table]], it happens away from the table — a few minutes at the end of a session, or during the week. Never as an authoring surface open during play.

---

## The dwell hazard

Beating a notebook on latency is a low bar, honestly stated. So **slowness is not the real failure mode.** The real one is the surface becoming somewhere a player *dwells*.

Reading is bounded — it ends when the answer is found. Browsing is not. A memory bank that is pleasant to scroll has become exactly the thing the constraint prohibits, no matter how fast each individual query is.

**Design consequence: answer-shaped, not document-shaped.** The player lands on what they asked for, not on a page they then read down. This is a deliberate divergence from the GM planning surface, which is optimised for wandering precisely because wandering is the point there.

---

## Why player search is a trustworthy signal

The behaviour **predates the tool.** Players already look things up; the tool only makes the lookup legible for the first time.

That is what makes it a sound **Investment** signal rather than a suspect one. It is not a new behaviour the tool induced in order to measure it, and it costs the player nothing extra to produce.

Limit restated so it is not oversold, per [[Live-Set]]: search reflects what the party **cannot remember**, which correlates with investment but is not identical to it. A well-remembered obsession generates no queries. A floor, not a measure.

## Open

1. **Does answer-shaped retrieval conflict with the memory-bank framing?** A player asking *what do we know about the Warden* wants a small set, not one fact. The bound may need to be on depth rather than on result count.
2. **Is there a legitimate player-side browse case at all** — session recap before play starts, for instance — and if so, is it a different surface rather than a mode of the same one?
