# Scope

What this tool is and isn't. Referenced from [[North-Star]] and [[Open-Requirements]].

---

## The purpose

**Facilitate play at the table.** Everything else is instrumental.

The tool exists so the GM can prepare efficiently, react confidently, and keep a story coherent across months of play. It is a narrative instrument.

---

## Out of scope

**Character sheets, stats, and progression.** HP, mana, levels, gear, modifiers. These live wherever they already live. The tool doesn't track or update them.

**Rules enforcement.** No validating that mob counts match tiers, that difficulties are set correctly, or that an encounter is mechanically sound. The GM knows the rules.

**Rules lookup.** Not a searchable rulebook. [[System-Reference]] holds mechanics as shared reference for the table, not as a query surface.

**Producing a written story.** No auto-generated prose narrative, no publishable campaign chronicle. The record serves the next session, not a reader.

**Combat resolution, dice, initiative.** Not a play aid in that sense.

---

## Where mechanics do matter

Only where they carry narrative weight.

*"Hilda used persuasion to turn the Warden at the moment Z's aggression had closed every other door"* is worth capturing. It's a character acting characteristically, at a hinge point, with consequences.

*"Hilda's persuasion modifier is +3"* is not.

The distinction: **the use of a capability at a significant moment is narrative. The capability's numbers are not.**

### Descriptive knowledge of abilities is useful

Knowing what a skill or spell *does*, described rather than quantified, adds to the story:

- *Frost Scar* leaves a lingering chill — so the room, the target, and the aftermath can be described
- Trick archery is a performance skill — so its use is showy, watched, and read by onlookers
- Improvised weapons means Z fights with whatever's at hand — which shapes how a fight is narrated

This kind of description belongs in the record. Damage dice don't.

---

## Corrections to earlier framing

**"Session records are chapters, not minutes"** — written in [[North-Star]], and wrong. It implies the record is meant to be read as narrative. It isn't. Records exist to make the next session better and to support the GM's own recounting at the table. Any framing that points toward producing a readable artifact is out of scope.

**The debrief is something the GM does, not something the tool produces.** Recounting what was behind the screen is a pleasure of running the game. The tool's job is to have the material retrievable — the abandoned branches, the projections, the manner notes — not to write the retelling.

---

## The test

For any proposed capability:

> Does this help at the table, or in preparing for the table?

If it produces an artifact for reading rather than for playing, it's out of scope.
