# Epic 3 — Know the next session is covered

Terms marked on first use are defined in [[Glossary]].

---

## Who it is for

The **game master**, during the week between sessions, preparing in whatever fragments of time exist.

They are trying to answer one question — *am I ready?* — and to trust the answer enough to stop.

---

## The problem

Preparation is not a volume problem. It is a **coverage** problem: the party can go in several directions, and the GM needs something behind each of the ones they might plausibly take.

The difficulty is that the directions are not written down anywhere as a set. They accumulate — a thread from three sessions ago, a name someone mentioned, a place the party said they would return to — and the GM holds them all in their head, imperfectly.

**A vignette.** The GM spends the week preparing the confrontation the party has clearly been building toward. It is good work: motivations, complications, several ways it could go.

The party opens the session by going back to the market to find a fence they heard about two sessions ago. The GM wrote the fence down at the time and left him as a name. There is nothing behind him.

Forty minutes of improvisation follow. It is fine. It is thinner than it should have been, and the thing the party was most interested in got the least attention — not through neglect, but because the GM was looking at the branch they expected rather than the whole surface.

The general shape: **the failure is not forgetting, it is not being able to see the whole surface at once.** Every open direction was written down. None of them were in view together.

And there is a second failure underneath it. A GM who cannot trust a readiness answer will keep preparing past the point of usefulness, which is its own cost — [[Arcs]] is explicit that preparing a full tree of possibilities is waste, since most of it never happens.

---

## What is not being asked for

- **Not proposing material.** Suggesting what should go behind an empty branch is Epic 6, and is blocked on an open decision about how far the tool may go.
- **Not inferring what the party cares about.** This epic uses only what the GM explicitly recorded — including what they noted the party returned to unprompted. Reading investment from patterns is Epic 6.
- **Not authoring.** Filling a gap is Epic 4.
- **Not judging quality.** Whether what is prepared is any good is the GM's business.
- **Not scheduling or reminding.** This answers a question when asked; it does not manage the GM's week.

## Value, and the cost of omission

**This is the epic that changes how the week feels.** Epics 1 and 2 make the record worth keeping and worth consulting. This one is where it starts doing work the GM could not do themselves — holding the whole open surface at once, which is precisely what a person cannot do.

Three costs:

**Preparation goes to the wrong place.** Not through carelessness — through looking at the expected branch rather than all of them.

**Preparation does not stop.** Without a trustworthy answer, the only safe move is to keep going, and the week has other things in it.

**The thin moments land on the threads that matter most.** The direction the party chooses is usually the one they care about, which makes an uncovered branch disproportionately expensive.

## Stories

### S1 — Tell me whether I am ready, and show me what you checked

*As the GM, I want a direct answer about the coming session, together with what it was based on, so that I can either trust it or see why not to.*

- **Outcome:** The GM can stop preparing on the strength of the answer.
- **Assertion:** A readiness answer always presents what was examined to produce it. It is never a bare verdict, a score, or a percentage.
- **Demo:** Ask against a fixture; the answer and its basis appear together, and the basis can be inspected item by item.

**Everything else here supports this story.** It is the one the GM actually wants.

### S2 — Show me every direction they might go

*As the GM, I want the open directions gathered in one place, because I cannot hold them all at once and they are what determines whether I am ready.*

- **Outcome:** The whole open surface is visible together rather than recalled piecemeal.
- **Assertion:** Open directions are assembled from what was recorded — unresolved threads, stated intentions, things the party returned to unprompted — and each shows where it came from.
- **Demo:** Show the open set for a fixture; trace three entries back to the sessions that produced them.

### S3 — Show me which of them have nothing behind them

*As the GM, I want to see the directions with no material prepared, so that my attention goes where it is missing rather than where it is comfortable.*

- **Outcome:** Uncovered branches are visible before the session, not discovered during it.
- **Assertion:** Each open direction is shown with what exists behind it and at what depth. An empty one is stated plainly.
- **Demo:** Show a fixture with a mix of covered and empty branches; the empty ones are identifiable at a glance.

### S4 — Let me say that one is deliberately thin

*As the GM, I want to mark a direction as intentionally unprepared, so it stops being raised and so my readiness answer stays honest.*

- **Outcome:** A deliberate choice is recorded as a choice, and the tool respects it.
- **Assertion:** A branch can be marked as deliberately thin, with the decision recorded. It is excluded from gap reporting and shown as a decision, not as coverage.
- **Demo:** Mark one; it disappears from the gaps and appears in the basis as a decision.

**This is what makes S1 trustworthy.** A coverage claim that cannot tell *deliberately thin* from *overlooked* is not one the GM can act on.

### S5 — Remind me what they left unfinished

*As the GM, I want the things the party left open in play surfaced during prep, because those are the directions they are most likely to take.*

- **Outcome:** Unresolved threads from play reach the prep week rather than waiting to be remembered.
- **Assertion:** Anything recorded as unresolved, or recorded as returned to unprompted, appears among the open directions with the session it came from.
- **Demo:** Record an unresolved thread in a session; it appears in the next prep view, traceable to its origin.

### S6 — Remind me what I said I would follow up on

*As the GM, I want my own notes to myself to come back at the right moment, since I write them precisely because I will forget.*

- **Outcome:** Intentions recorded after a session return during the week they matter.
- **Assertion:** Follow-ups recorded against a session appear in the next prep view and can be closed, deferred, or dropped, with the outcome recorded.
- **Demo:** Record a follow-up; resolve it three ways across three runs; each outcome is visible.

### S7 — Tell me when I have prepared enough

*As the GM, I want to know when a branch has enough behind it, so that I stop rather than building a tree that will never be used.*

- **Outcome:** The GM stops at sufficient rather than at exhausted.
- **Assertion:** Depth is reported against a stated expectation — how far ahead coverage is expected to reach — and further depth is shown as beyond it, not as better.
- **Demo:** Show a branch prepared past the expectation; it reads as covered, not as more covered.

### S8 — Do not tell me I am behind because fields are empty

*As the GM, I want blanks treated as normal, because most of them are correct and a tool that nags about them will be ignored.*

- **Outcome:** The readiness answer reflects the coming session, not the tidiness of the record.
- **Assertion:** No completeness score, progress bar, or prompt to fill appears anywhere in this epic's output. An unfilled optional field never contributes to a gap.
- **Demo:** Run readiness against a deliberately sparse fixture. It reports ready, and nothing suggests otherwise.

### S9 — Let me check in fragments across the week

*As the GM, I want to look at this in ten minutes on a Tuesday, because that is when preparation actually happens.*

- **Outcome:** Readiness is usable in short visits rather than requiring a sitting.
- **Assertion:** The view opens directly to what is open and what is missing, needs no setup step, and reflects any change made since the last visit.
- **Demo:** Make a change, reopen, and see it reflected without any intervening action.

## Open questions

| Question | What it blocks | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| What makes a direction *live*? How long does a thread the party dropped stay open? | S2's contents | Needs a stated basis, even a crude one. Likely refined by observation |
| Is readiness one answer or a list? *Ready* is what the GM wants; a list may be what is honest | S1's shape | Design question, deliberately left open |
| How far ahead is coverage expected to reach? | S7's assertion | [[Arcs]] says one or two branches. Confirm it as the stated expectation |
| Does something the party returned to unprompted weigh more heavily than an ordinary open thread? | S5 quality | Weighting edges toward inference. Keep it visible and GM-adjustable rather than automatic |

## Dependencies

- **Epic 1** records unresolved threads, follow-ups, and resurfacing. Without those, S5 and S6 have no input and S2's open set is thin.
- **Epic 13** puts the existing campaign in. Readiness over an empty store reports everything as uncovered.
- **Epic 4** is where a gap gets filled. This epic reports; it does not fix.
- **Prerequisite P3.** The fixture needs a realistic spread of covered, empty, and deliberately thin branches, or S3 and S4 cannot be exercised.
